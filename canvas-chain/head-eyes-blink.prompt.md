Inside this Lab, render and inspect its actual exported and reimported candidate, using matched source/candidate cameras, lighting, framing and materials. Produce actual animation-cycle videos from front, oblique and profile views, plus eye close-up evidence. Each video must show neutral, intermediate closure, full closure, and reopening, for each side independently and both sides together. Use enough frames to reveal a pop or sudden change between construction knots; stills alone do not satisfy motion verification. Preserve matched stills, material ownership views and shading ablations when needed to identify whether a visible defect comes from skin, lid, band, accessory or shading.

Evaluate these visual conditions explicitly:
- Neutral identity: the face, original band placement, colors and materials match the incoming neutral reference; reopening returns to that same appearance.
- Lid appearance: real lid surfaces close over the eye without a visible remaining aperture, stray eye sliver, folded sheet, black strip or abrupt material seam. Keep the original eyeballs still.
- Lash motion: the original upper band and accessories visibly follow the closing lid. Check for a stationary lash left above the closed eye, detached or floating roots, tears, spikes, distracting stretching, and accessories separating from their parent band.
- Profile and oblique shape: the eyelid/lash silhouette remains plausible, without an obvious projecting flap, inverted ribbon, visible gap or lash passing through the face. Front-view coverage alone is insufficient.
- Temporal behavior: closing and reopening are smooth, with no snapping, popping, abrupt silhouette reversal, visible self-crossing or loss of contact between frames. Check independent blinks as well as bilateral motion.

Visual judgment must be based on the rendered evidence, not a numeric mask score. Record each view/control sequence inspected and explain any remaining visible limitation. Numeric failures may help locate a visible defect, but they cannot alone override a visually correct result. A numeric pass likewise cannot excuse a visible defect. If appearance cannot be assessed, report visual verification as incomplete rather than assuming acceptance.
