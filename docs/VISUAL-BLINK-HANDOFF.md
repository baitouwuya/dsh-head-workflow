# Visual blink diagnosis and native handoff

The preceding native pass certified all 423 measured boundary segments, then
stopped at original right-lash contact: one of 379 band vertices was inside the
declared contact limit. The failed native evidence remains unchanged. No accepted
replacement model follows from that numerical continuity result.

## Reproduced causes

The generated neutral moving rail was substantially separated from the original
band even though the source band itself was unchanged. The old construction
carried projected skin-rim depth across the lid section. Fitting the generated
rail to the actual source band improves contact without moving the original
neutral source. Contact count and span remain reported diagnostics.

Full textured host review exposed two independent striping causes. Direct skin
samples included baked eyeliner; a robust low-frequency field from surrounding
skin avoids carrying that pigment into new surfaces. More seriously, writing
posed normals onto the shape-key Basis caused Blender to compensate for the
deformation twice. Static posed geometry and automatic-normal ablations isolated
that error. Initialize neutral normals once, then let the actual morph geometry
and exported normal targets evaluate. The original encoded images stay intact.

The original curve transfer rotates the material plane along the arch but does
not supply a deliberate roll around the local lateral tangent as the lid closes.
A forward ribbon roll reduces the broad closed-band silhouette in host review.
Whole bands and their accessories still require actual carrier and motion checks;
a plausible front view does not certify the profile or temporal behavior.

The closed capsule also produced projecting pale surfaces. Carrying a constant
rim-depth floor across the interior, independently closing upper/lower rails at
different depths, and nonplanar corner fans all contributed. A shared closure
curve and an open front surface based on current-pose eye depth reduce those
specific defects. Reusing irregular capsule station mappings can still produce
folds: duplicate outer stations and a large source-boundary jump were measured.
Pointwise eye clearance does not certify triangles between the vertices; actual
ownership renders revealed small eye slivers through a point-cleared prototype.

The bounded route reparameterization reduced the largest upper station jump
from about 0.02006 to 0.00162 in the measured normalized frame. Upper-dominant
closure reduced the large lower sheet, but did not establish a visually correct
blink. At full closure, 94 right and 83 left band vertices became newly occluded
behind the original skin compared with neutral. Neutral already had 62 and 58
embedded vertices, respectively; inherited intersections are reported separately.
This is a vertex-projection diagnostic, not a complete triangle collision test.
It explains the apparently severed outer wings seen in the host renders.

The constrained triangle-clearance attempt is retained as failed evidence. It
required excessive forward offsets, including about 0.1477 on one upper lid,
and still had fixed-constraint shortfalls. Those arrays are not the native
construction default. Do not reinterpret that failure as a clearance pass or
carry its distorted geometry into the next build.

Stationary dark marks above a closing lash are present in the original skin
texture. Ownership renders distinguish them from generated striping. Any
source-derived cover surface must preserve the original image and geometry,
remain hidden at neutral, and be reviewed throughout closing and reopening.
Neither a numeric mask score nor hiding a defect in a still image is acceptance.

## Evidence and acceptance

Host scenes, array caches, comparative renders and videos are prechecks only.
Their geometry is never uploaded or substituted for the canvas Lab build.
The supplied source helpers are reused inside the native Lab against the actual
connected input, with runtime-derived roles, frame, configuration and provenance.
The immutable base recipe and exact correction are verified and tested there.

The [current native verification contract](NATIVE-BLINK-VERIFICATION.md) replaces
earlier numeric publication gates. Preserve every numerical failure as a measured
diagnostic. Invalid or unavailable construction data is a concrete technical
failure, but a threshold exceedance alone must not suppress a renderable result.

The [native export helpers](NATIVE-FULL-LOOK-EXPORT.md) seal the Lab's own GLB and
editable scene, retain the original embedded image bytes, serialize independent
and bilateral blink clips, then reimport and render the sealed file's actual
animation channels. The videos include between-key samples at the correct time
base. A common head assembly retains source hierarchy and generated attachments;
rigged inputs require a measured head-bone binding.

Review the Lab's own front, oblique and profile motion, including neutral return
and eye close-ups. Publish its primary artifact last and leave it selected on the
authorized canvas only when those renders look correct. Preserve any remaining
visible failure and stop without another native attempt. The current diagnosis
and source tests are not a claim that a native replacement has been accepted.

## Native result: visual rejection, evidence retained

The single native follow-up built from correction
`396f9ad7051eaf2b3b130cbffd890edd04b08523` over the immutable base. Base tests
passed (six Python and four Node); correction tests passed (208 Python and four
Node); the final native adapter suites passed (212 Python and four Node).
The provided runtime was Blender 4.2.9 LTS. Both construction entrypoints ran
once. Three subsequent export assemblies reused the same constructed arrays to
repair compatibility and packed-image serialization; no second construction ran.

The reimported native model failed visible-motion acceptance. Its right-eye
front, oblique, profile and close-up sequences show disappearing outer lash
attachment, an exaggerated bowed ribbon and an implausible projecting lid
profile during late closure. Neutral appearance and reopening match the input
in the completed views. These visible defects, rather than numeric thresholds,
are the reason for rejection.

Measured moving contacts increased to five on the right and seven on the left.
Source neutral/protected displacement and eye displacement remained zero. Yet
82 right and 126 left band vertices became newly occluded behind source skin
at full closure, beyond the inherited 62 and 58. Maximum band edge expansion
was about 2.190 and 1.983. These measurements are diagnostic, not acceptance
rules. The native bounded target-depth adapter did not solve final band/skin
occlusion or the coupled silhouette problem.

Four right-eye videos completed, each with 46 frames at 48 fps. Rendering then
terminated with exit code 143; the cause was not supplied. Only three left-eye
front frames survived, and no bilateral cycle completed. Ownership/shading
ablations and the final verifier report remained incomplete. Per-frame report
checkpointing remains an unimplemented limitation.

The diagnostic GLB contains 15 meshes, a common head assembly and three actual
blink clips. Its source was an unrigged Smart Mesh FBX, so it has no skins and
eight blink target names; it is not the frozen accepted head's rig/morph
contract. Its original 721,553-byte JPEG is preserved exactly. The rejected
native candidate and editable scene are retained inside the evidence archive:

only the failure-evidence archive: no primary model was published or selected,
and no further native attempt or downstream stage was started.
